Pool acid wash

When stains and discoloration have set into the plaster, an acid wash can bring back a brighter finish. The pool is drained and a thin layer of the surface is cleaned away to reveal the fresher plaster underneath.

Get a free quote (818) 857-1463

When an acid wash makes sense

  • Algae or mineral stains that don't come out with brushing or treatment
  • Dull, gray or blotchy plaster
  • After a severe green pool or long period of neglect
  • High dissolved solids that call for draining and refilling anyway

How an acid wash works

  1. Drain the pool safely and in line with local rules for draining.
  2. Clean out debris from the floor and drains.
  3. Acid wash the surface in sections and rinse thoroughly.
  4. Neutralize and clean up the wash water.
  5. Refill and start up with balanced water chemistry.

Is an acid wash right for your pool?

Every acid wash removes a thin layer of plaster, so it isn't something to repeat often. If the plaster is thin, rough or delaminating, replastering may be the better investment. We'll look at the surface and tell you honestly which makes sense.

Common questions

How often can a pool be acid washed?

Sparingly. Because each wash removes some plaster, it's best saved for when stains can't be removed any other way.

Will an acid wash remove all stains?

It removes many surface stains, but some deep metal or organic stains may remain. We'll set expectations before we start.

How long is the pool out of service?

Draining, washing and refilling are usually done over a few days, depending on the pool size and fill rate.
