Pool replastering & resurfacing

Plaster doesn't last forever. When the surface is rough on your feet, etched, chalky or permanently stained, replastering gives the pool a smooth new finish and a fresh look.

Get a free quote (818) 857-1463

Signs it's time to replaster

  • Rough plaster that scratches feet or snags swimsuits
  • Etching, pitting or chalky residue
  • Stains that won't come out with an acid wash
  • Hollow spots, delamination or exposed gunite
  • Cracks in the surface

What the project involves

  1. Drain and prep. The pool is drained and the old surface is prepared so the new plaster bonds well.
  2. Tile and fittings. A good time to address tile repair and replace fittings if needed.
  3. Apply the new finish. Fresh plaster or an upgraded finish is applied and smoothed.
  4. Fill and start up. The pool is refilled and the new surface is carefully brought up with a proper start-up chemistry plan.

Finish options

Classic white plaster gives the familiar bright blue water. Colored plasters and aggregate finishes (quartz or pebble) can last longer and change the water color. We'll walk you through the options and what fits your budget.

Start-up care matters. New plaster needs closely managed water chemistry for its first weeks, and that's exactly what weekly service is for.

Common questions

How long does pool plaster last?

Standard white plaster often lasts around 10 to 15 years with good water chemistry. Aggregate finishes can last longer.

How long does replastering take?

Most projects take about one to two weeks from draining to refill, depending on the pool and the finish.

Should I fix tile at the same time?

If tile is loose or damaged, replastering is the ideal time, since the pool is already drained.
